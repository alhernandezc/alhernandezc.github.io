Adriana L. Hernández Castañeda

Economist & demographer: labor, migration, and gender, across places.

I study how labor markets, migration, and gender inequality unfold geographically, using survey microdata, administrative records, and maps. I've built population estimates used by New York State agencies, co-authored the UC Berkeley Labor Center's statewide study of civil-service vacancies, and published peer-reviewed research on the changing sex composition of US immigrant communities.

I'm completing a PhD at the University of California, Davis, and was a 2025 Fulbright García Robles finalist. I work natively in Spanish and English.

AboutTrained in economics, working across borders

I was trained in economics (BA, Sonoma State University; MS, University of Nevada, Reno) and I'm now completing a PhD at UC Davis, where my research combines econometrics with spatial demography; the maps matter as much as the regressions.

At Cornell University's Program on Applied Demographics (2018–2021), I produced demographic analysis supporting New York State's official population-estimates program, authored memos on household formation and population-estimate revisions, and presented statistical-area updates to the New York State Department of Labor. At the UC Berkeley Labor Center (2022–2023), I co-authored Civil Service Vacancies in California: 2022–2023, a statewide study of public-sector hiring challenges and the policies that could fix them.

My current research spans both sides of the US–Mexico relationship, and my portfolio is moving into the open: public data, public write-ups, and code releases rolling out through 2026 on the projects page.
